PLANT: grab a gum, wattle or grass. MORE PLANTS offers banksia, sedge and litter. Open over a clear patch to plant; hold the blue can nearby to establish it.

CREEK: drop blue stones to extend the channel. Enable EDIT MAP, grab an existing miniature blue stone and release it elsewhere to reshape the creek. Waterholes must connect to the channel.

RAIN LAB: plant ground cover in plot B, predict which plot will lose more soil, then run a fair test. Compare simulated runoff, infiltration and soil loss. Clouds also let you rain locally.

CONNECT: establish ground cover in the four gold approach rings, place the bridge at the gold road crossing, and test the wildlife route. Shrubs at the outer approaches add shelter for the model skink route.

KANGAROOS: grab a grass bundle, make a gentle underarm toss and open your hand. Kangaroos approach reachable food and eat it. Feeding is pretend play only—do not feed or throw objects at real wildlife.

EDIT MAP: the miniature is linked to the room. Release a building tool above a miniature location to work at a distance. Blue control stones are editable on the CREEK page. No extra walking or crouching is required.

How grabbing and watering work
01Reach and close. Reach for the root plug or anywhere on the blue can. Pinch, or close your fingers as though picking it up. A bright halo and a soft note confirm your target.02Open to plant. Move the seedling away from its shelf. A green ring shows the planting spot. Open your hand; the seedling floats down. No bending or throwing.03Hold to water. Bring the can near a thirsty tree. In Easy watering mode it tips and pours automatically. A blue ring confirms the target. Keep holding until the tree grows, then open your hand to return the can.

Walk, then stop: the tools catch up when you move away and lower your empty hands. The forest stays planted. To call your tools, hold an empty open palm facing upwards at waist height for one second, away from objects. The gold rail also moves the nursery.

Brief tracking loss: the game pauses pouring and holds your item temporarily, instead of dropping it. Bring your hand back into view. After a longer loss it safely returns the item without planting it.

Room view: touch and hold the wide ROOM VIEW tile to hide the forest. Touch again to resume. RESET requires a two-second hold. Buttons use a visible progress indicator; objects do not require a button.

Quest / ManageXR setup & room scale

Host this HTML on your existing HTTPS GitHub Pages site and launch it from the configured WebXR browser. Enable hand tracking and select Enter mixed reality. Opening a normal browser tab is not entering mixed reality.

The game uses floor-relative tracking, with no fixed arena or starting-point radius. Walk only inside a clear, supervised area approved by your school. The headset’s safety boundary remains in charge. A stationary boundary must be changed by your authorised administrator for classroom-sized movement; this page cannot expand or disable it.

Keep your floor height calibrated. Optional room surfaces help with occlusion and warn about mapped obstacles; they do not reliably detect all furniture or people. Missing floor scans do not lock planting to a tiny patch. Never lean on virtual objects. Keep hands in view and use ROOM VIEW before moving through a cluttered area.

Learning model, sources and limitations

Plants and wildlife are stylised examples of an Australian eucalypt woodland / creek mosaic, not an exact reconstruction of a named ecological community. Growth is accelerated. Existing trees do not count as newly established layers.

Rainfall trials use a simplified equal-area soil-cover model. Rain level, slope and initial soil moisture are held equal between plots; plant cover is sampled at the start. Percentages describe model water shares, and the soil-loss index is unitless—not measured litres, millimetres or real flood predictions. River flow follows the designed channel, not the real classroom floor slope. Vegetation does not prevent all flooding, and sediment colour is not a complete water-quality test.

The corridor challenge is a simplified connectivity exercise, not a wildlife engineering design. Feeding is a fictional interaction, not real wildlife-care advice. Read the Australian Museum’s eastern grey kangaroo guide, NSW riparian vegetation guidance and WaterNSW vegetation-layer guidance.

Each headset has its own landscape: no multiplayer or shared anchoring. No accounts, camera recording, analytics or runtime downloads. Settings stay locally in this browser. Plant positions and evidence reset with a new world or a new XR session. Optional room scans are not reliable obstacle detection. Keep the headset boundary enabled and supervise use.
